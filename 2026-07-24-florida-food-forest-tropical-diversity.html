The Story

A food forest does not need to occupy several acres to contain remarkable diversity.

In this Florida garden, KC has used approximately 0.17 acres to grow an extensive collection of tropical fruit trees and edible plants. The tour offers a close look at how plants can be layered, grouped, and fitted into a residential landscape without treating each tree as an isolated specimen.

This section can later become the complete written story of the garden, including how KC began growing, how the property developed, the biggest challenges, and the ideas visitors can apply in their own yards.

What Makes This Garden Abundant?

The defining feature of this food forest is its density.

Rather than leaving large open spaces between individual trees, the garden uses vertical layers and close planting to create room for a much wider range of edible species. That approach allows a small property to function as an unusually diverse living collection.

Growing Lessons From This Tour

  1. Small properties can hold considerable diversity.
  2. Vertical layers help make better use of limited growing space.
  3. Dense planting can create a food-forest environment rather than a conventional orchard layout.
  4. Plant selection and placement matter when many species share a small area.
  5. A residential landscape can become both productive and highly diverse.
