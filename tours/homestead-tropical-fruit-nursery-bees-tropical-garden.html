Inside Beez Tropical Garden

Beez Tropical Garden is more than a tropical plant nursery. The property combines plant production with mature orchards, allowing visitors to see many of the same species growing as established fruiting trees before purchasing them for their own gardens. Throughout the tour, Bee shares practical growing advice while introducing dozens of edible and ornamental tropical plants collected from around the world. :contentReferenceoaicite:1

Walking through the property reveals mature longans, sapodillas, mangoes, starfruit, avocados, miracle fruit, tropical herbs, medicinal plants, perennial vegetables, and countless rare species that thrive in South Florida’s subtropical climate. The nursery demonstrates not only what can be grown in Homestead, but also how plant diversity creates resilient and productive edible landscapes. :contentReferenceoaicite:2

Highlights From Beez Tropical Garden

The Plant That Smells Like Root Beer

One of the nursery’s most memorable plants is the Root Beer Plant, an edible tropical herb whose leaves release a surprisingly authentic root beer aroma. Bee explains how the leaves can be used in cooking and demonstrates why unusual culinary plants like this have become favorites among tropical plant collectors. :contentReferenceoaicite:0

Cuban Oregano: Food and Traditional Medicine

Bee introduces Cuban oregano, a fragrant tropical herb valued both as a culinary ingredient and for its long history in traditional home remedies. Plants like this demonstrate how edible landscapes often combine food production with generations of practical plant knowledge. :contentReferenceoaicite:1

A Tropical Nursery Built Around Mature Fruit Trees

Many tropical nurseries display young plants growing in containers, requiring visitors to imagine what those species might look like years later. Beez Tropical Garden offers something different. Throughout the property, mature fruit trees stand alongside nursery stock, allowing visitors to see the full size, growth habit, and fruit production of many species before deciding what to plant at home. :contentReferenceoaicite:2

This combination of nursery and established orchard creates a valuable learning experience. Rather than relying solely on labels or photographs, visitors can compare juvenile plants with mature specimens while hearing firsthand how different species perform in South Florida’s climate. For gardeners planning their own edible landscapes, that perspective can make selecting the right tree much easier. :contentReferenceoaicite:3

As Bee guides the tour, each section of the property introduces another collection of tropical fruit trees, medicinal herbs, perennial vegetables, and ornamental edible plants. The result is less like walking through a retail nursery and more like exploring a living botanical collection where nearly every plant tells a story about food, culture, or tropical horticulture. :contentReferenceoaicite:4

Rare Tropical Fruits From Around the World

One of the defining features of Beez Tropical Garden is the diversity of tropical fruit species growing throughout the property. Mature longans, sapodillas, miracle fruit, mangoes, avocados, starfruit, and numerous lesser-known fruits illustrate the incredible range of edible plants that thrive in South Florida’s frost-free climate. Some trees have been growing for more than twenty years, offering visitors an opportunity to observe how these species develop over time. :contentReferenceoaicite:5

Rather than focusing on only a handful of commercial fruits, Bee emphasizes diversity. Every visit introduces plants that many gardeners have never encountered before, making the nursery an excellent destination for collectors interested in expanding both their knowledge and their home orchards. This commitment to plant diversity has helped establish Beez Tropical Garden as a destination for tropical fruit enthusiasts visiting Homestead. :contentReferenceoaicite:6

Medicinal Plants and Everyday Edibles

Although tropical fruit trees are the main attraction, Beez Tropical Garden also showcases a wide variety of medicinal herbs, culinary plants, and perennial vegetables that have been grown and used throughout tropical regions for generations. These plants demonstrate that an edible landscape can provide far more than fruit alone, supplying herbs, seasonings, teas, and traditional remedies alongside the orchard. :contentReferenceoaicite:0

Throughout the tour, Bee introduces plants such as Cuban oregano, the Root Beer Plant, pandan, and numerous other useful species that many visitors have never encountered before. Some are valued for their distinctive flavors, while others have long histories in traditional medicine or everyday cooking. Together, they highlight the remarkable diversity that can be incorporated into a tropical garden. :contentReferenceoaicite:1

For gardeners interested in creating more resilient edible landscapes, these smaller plants are often just as valuable as the fruit trees themselves. Many tolerate partial shade, produce year-round harvests, and fit easily into spaces beneath larger trees, allowing every layer of the landscape to become productive. :contentReferenceoaicite:2

Learning Through Experience

One of the greatest advantages of visiting Beez Tropical Garden is the opportunity to learn directly from someone who has spent decades growing tropical fruit in South Florida. Rather than simply displaying plants for sale, Bee explains how different species grow, what conditions they prefer, and how they fit into a productive tropical landscape. These firsthand observations provide practical knowledge that can’t always be found in books or online guides. :contentReferenceoaicite:3

Seeing mature specimens alongside nursery plants also helps visitors make more informed decisions. It’s one thing to purchase a small tree in a container, but it’s another to stand beneath a fully mature longan or sapodilla and understand the space, care, and harvest potential that tree represents. That perspective is one of the qualities that makes Beez Tropical Garden a valuable destination for both beginning gardeners and experienced tropical fruit collectors. :contentReferenceoaicite:4

The Green Yard Takeaway

Beez Tropical Garden demonstrates that one of the best ways to learn tropical fruit growing is by seeing mature trees in person. While books and videos provide valuable information, walking beneath a productive longan, tasting fruit directly from the tree, and hearing decades of growing experience from someone who cultivates these plants every day offers a completely different perspective.

Another lesson from this tour is the importance of diversity. Fruit trees, medicinal plants, culinary herbs, perennial vegetables, and ornamental edibles all contribute something unique to the landscape. Together they create a garden that is not only productive, but also educational, resilient, and endlessly interesting to explore.

Whether you’re planning a backyard orchard in South Florida or simply looking for inspiration, Beez Tropical Garden shows how curiosity, experimentation, and a passion for plants can grow into an extraordinary collection that continues teaching others year after year.
