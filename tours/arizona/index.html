Growing in Arizona

Growing Food in the Low Desert

Arizona includes very different growing environments, from the hot low desert to much cooler high-elevation regions. The Green Yard’s current Arizona tours are concentrated in the Phoenix metropolitan area, where growers are working with intense summer heat, limited rainfall, mild winters, and occasional freezes.

The landscapes featured here show how individual growers respond to those conditions in different ways. Shade, irrigation, mulch, canopy, plant placement, and microclimates all appear throughout the tours, but each grower brings their own experience and approach to creating a productive landscape in the desert.

Rather than presenting one formula for growing in Arizona, these tours are an opportunity to learn directly from the people doing the work: what they grow, what has succeeded, what has struggled, and how their landscapes continue to change through experience.

Climate Snapshot

Arizona Low-Desert Growing Context

These broad points provide context for the Phoenix-area low desert where The Green Yard’s current Arizona tours are located. The individual tours show how growers actually respond to these conditions on the ground.

Climate Hot Desert

Mediterranean, coastal, desert, mountain, and inland climates all occur within the state.

Growing Season Nearly Year-Round

Different crops thrive at different times of year, allowing growers to keep something productive through much of the calendar.

Summer Extreme Heat

Phoenix summers regularly bring extreme heat, creating one of the biggest challenges for young and heat-sensitive plants.

Winter Mild With Freeze Risk

Most winter days are mild, but occasional cold nights and freezes can challenge tropical and subtropical plants.

Water Limited Rainfall

Growers depend heavily on irrigation while using mulch, soil-building, and other approaches to help conserve moisture.

Common Theme Microclimates

The tours repeatedly show growers using shade, canopy, structures, and plant placement to create different conditions within one property.

The Growing Reality

What Arizona Growers Are Working With

Opportunities Growers Explore

  • Abundant sunshine and a long overall growing season
  • Established success with citrus, dates, figs, pomegranates, and many other fruits
  • Mild winters allow growers to experiment with subtropical plants
  • Dense planting and canopy can create increasingly protected spaces
  • Local growers continue testing what can succeed in the desert

Growing Challenges

  • Extreme summer heat and intense afternoon sun
  • Very low natural rainfall and significant irrigation needs
  • Alkaline soils and water can create additional growing challenges
  • Occasional winter freezes can damage sensitive plants
  • Young trees may need extra protection while becoming established
