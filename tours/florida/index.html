<div class="gy-kicker">Growing in Florida</div>

<h2 class="gy-section-title">
  One State.<br>
  Very Different Growing Conditions.
</h2>

<p class="gy-section-intro">
  These tours don't provide one formula for growing in Florida.
  Instead, they show how growers adapt to the conditions around them —
  from Tampa and Central Florida to the Atlantic coast, Miami, and
  Homestead.
</p>

<div class="gy-context-grid">

  <div class="gy-context-card">
    <h3>Warmth</h3>
    <p>
      Florida's climate creates opportunities for tropical and
      subtropical plants that would be difficult to grow across much
      of the continental United States.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="gy-context-card">
    <h3>Cold</h3>
    <p>
      Warm does not mean frost-free everywhere. Rebel Farms shows
      how a major freeze can quickly reveal which species and
      cultivars are actually resilient.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="gy-context-card">
    <h3>Water</h3>
    <p>
      Florida growers may deal with heavy rainfall, flooding, drought
      periods, and intense humidity — sometimes within the same
      growing system.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="gy-context-card">
    <h3>Soil</h3>
    <p>
      Across these properties, growers use mulch, compost, cover
      crops, chop-and-drop, living roots, and other strategies to
      continually build their soils.
    </p>
  </div>

</div>

<div class="gy-kicker">What We're Learning</div>

<h2 class="gy-section-title">
  Lessons From<br>
  Florida Growers
</h2>

<p class="gy-section-intro">
  Every property is different, but several ideas continue to appear
  as we document how people are producing food across Florida.
</p>

<div class="gy-lessons-grid">

  <div class="gy-lesson">
    <div class="gy-lesson-number">01</div>
    <h3>Diversity Creates Options</h3>
    <p>
      Growing many species and cultivars allows growers to see what
      succeeds when weather, pests, or other conditions change.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="gy-lesson">
    <div class="gy-lesson-number">02</div>
    <h3>Build the Soil</h3>
    <p>
      Cover crops, mulch, compost, living roots, and chop-and-drop
      appear repeatedly across very different Florida systems.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="gy-lesson">
    <div class="gy-lesson-number">03</div>
    <h3>Test Before You Scale</h3>
    <p>
      Real-world plant performance can matter more than what a label
      says should grow in a particular climate.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="gy-lesson">
    <div class="gy-lesson-number">04</div>
    <h3>Use the Whole Property</h3>
    <p>
      Front yards, side yards, native canopy, ponds, fences, and
      small pockets of sunlight can all become productive spaces.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="gy-lesson">
    <div class="gy-lesson-number">05</div>
    <h3>Grow for Your Microclimate</h3>
    <p>
      Florida isn't one growing environment. Coastal exposure,
      freezes, flooding, shade, and urban heat can change what works.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="gy-lesson">
    <div class="gy-lesson-number">06</div>
    <h3>Abundance Can Be Shared</h3>
    <p>
      CSAs, neighbors, volunteers, community gardens, nurseries, and
      shared harvests show that these landscapes can reach beyond a
      single household.
    </p>
  </div>
